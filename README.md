# Verified Stallings folding for finitely generated subgroups of F₂

This Lean project formalizes an executable construction of a finite inverse
automaton for every finitely generated subgroup of
`FreeGroup (Fin 2) ≃ F(a,b)`. Given a finite list of signed words, it builds the
flower graph, folds it, and proves that traversal of the canonical reduced
word for `g` succeeds exactly when `g` belongs to the subgroup generated by the
input list.

The Palomar statement is the finite-recognizer theorem in
[`Challenge.lean`](Challenge.lean). It imports only Mathlib. The verified
witness and proof are in [`Solution.lean`](Solution.lean), using the standalone
implementation under `Stallings/`.

## Construction

`Stallings/InverseAutomaton.lean` defines finite partial inverse automata and
proves canonical-word membership testing. `Stallings/Folding.lean` defines a
finite inverse multigraph and computes the least deterministic fold
congruence by exhaustive search over Boolean vertex relations. The search is
finite and executable, though much less efficient than union-find.

`Stallings/Flower.lean` builds the initial loops from a list of words and
proves the end-to-end results:

- `foldWords S` has based-loop subgroup exactly `generatorSubgroup S`;
- `foldedMembershipTest S g = true` iff `g ∈ generatorSubgroup S`;
- `folded_recognizer_exists` packages that construction as a finite automaton
  for Palomar.

The state type retains auxiliary isolated states to keep the construction
computable with a simple finite type. They cannot be reached from the basepoint
and do not affect accepted words. The package does not yet trim the core or
compute subgroup bases, rank, intersections, finite index, or index.

## Research context and scope

The compared theorem is the classical finite-graph recognition result behind
Stallings folding, not a claim of a new group-theoretic theorem. Stallings
introduced the finite-graph method for finitely generated subgroups of free
groups; Kapovich and Myasnikov later developed its combinatorial and
algorithmic role in subgroup theory. This project makes the construction
executable in Lean and proves that its output recognizes exactly the subgroup
generated by any input list in `F₂`. That verified membership procedure is the
formal contribution. It is relevant to computational group theory and
formal verification of algorithms, while remaining deliberately narrower than
the surrounding theory: the fold is found by exhaustive finite search, and
there are no complexity bounds or formalized core-pruning, basis,
intersection, or index algorithms.

## Build and local release checks

From this directory:

```sh
lake exe cache get
lake build
lake env lean scripts/StatementAudit.lean
```

The project pins Lean 4.33.0 and Mathlib commit
`db584cd6d46c92f209a44c0f1c829460d327499d`. The Comparator configuration is
[`comparator.json`](comparator.json); it selects the Challenge theorem and its
closed mathematical statement definition, with NanoDa enabled.

Palomar Comparator/NanoDa verification and the exact Linux hosted Challenge
renderer replay are separate release gates. The project is prepared as a new
Palomar entry; no Palomar intake or registration has been initiated.

## Mathematical source and provenance

The construction follows John R. Stallings, “Topology of Finite Graphs,”
*Inventiones Mathematicae* 71 (1983), 551–565,
[doi:10.1007/BF02095993](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02095993). The package uses
Mathlib's free-group quotient and reduced-word normal form; the structured
record is in [`formalization.yaml`](formalization.yaml), with additional
provenance notes in [`PROVENANCE.md`](PROVENANCE.md).

For the later computational treatment of subgroup graphs and their
algorithmic consequences, see Ilya Kapovich and Alexei Myasnikov,
“Stallings foldings and the subgroup structure of free groups,”
*Journal of Algebra* 248 (2002), 608–668,
[doi:10.1006/jabr.2001.9033](https://doi.org/10.1006/jabr.2001.9033).
